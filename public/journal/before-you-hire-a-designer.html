Before you hire a designer: seven decisions for your first cafe

The best brief a first-time owner can bring answers seven questions. None of them are about colour.

date
17 November 2026
read
5 min
by
Shirin KC

Most first-time cafe owners come to a designer with a mood board. It matters, but it is the eighth thing we need. The seven before it decide the size of the kitchen, the number of seats, how long the build takes and how far the budget goes.

Settle them first and design moves faster and costs less, because nothing gets drawn twice. On a low budget, that is where the savings are.

1. Your menu decides your kitchen

A pour-over bar needs a counter, a grinder and a sink. An all-day brunch menu needs a hot kitchen, an exhaust, cold storage and a place to wash up, and it takes far more of the floor. Decide the menu, then the equipment list, then the plan, in that order. Changing the menu after the kitchen is drawn is one of the most expensive changes you can make.

The design question: what is on the menu on day one, and what might you add in year two?

2. How many seats, and for how long

Is your cafe mostly takeaway, quick stops, or long visits with a laptop? The answer sets the seat count, the table sizes and how long the counter needs to be. Write down a rough split: how many customers stay ten minutes, and how many stay an hour. Our post on designing a small cafe on a budget shows how that split turns into seating.

3. Check the site before you sign

The expensive surprises sit in the building, not in the design. Before you sign, check:

  1. How much electrical load the site has. A commercial coffee machine and kitchen draw far more than a shop.
  2. Water supply, and a drain where the kitchen will go.
  3. A route for the kitchen exhaust to reach the outside.
  4. The ceiling height, and what sits above it.
  5. How visible the shopfront is from the street, and whether you can put up a sign.
  6. Any outdoor space, and whether you are allowed to seat people there.

Bring a designer or contractor to the site before you sign. An hour there can save months later.

4. Read the landlord's terms

How long is the rent-free fit-out period? Can you alter walls, floors, the shopfront or the roof? Who pays to upgrade power and water? What are the rules on signage, and what must you restore when you leave? Each answer changes the design and the budget, so read the lease before the first drawing, not after.

5. Split the budget, don't guess it

Skip the cost per square foot for now. Divide the money into four parts instead:

PartWhat it covers
EquipmentCoffee machine, kitchen, refrigeration, billing
InteriorsCivil work, electrical and plumbing, furniture, lighting, signage
Licences and feesFSSAI food licence, trade licence and anything your site needs
ContingencyWhat the site reveals once work begins

On a low budget, the order matters. Equipment comes first because the menu depends on it, then the interiors that make the cafe work, then the finishes. The contingency is the one you never spend on day one.

6. The feeling, in one sentence

Before you look at a single reference picture, write one sentence about how your cafe should feel at its best hour. "A bright corner where office workers get their first coffee in five minutes" leads to a very different room than "a slow, green place to spend a Sunday afternoon".

Write one sentence about how your cafe should feel at its best hour. Every decision gets checked against it.

That sentence is what the colours, materials and lights all answer to. It is also what keeps a small budget from being spent on things that don't serve it.

7. Work backwards from opening day

The rough order is design and drawings, then approvals and licences, then the build, then equipment delivery and installation, then a trial run with friends before you open. Each step waits on the one before, and most delays come from a decision that arrives late. Pick your opening date, count back, and you will see when each of the six decisions above is needed.

Bring these seven answers to your first meeting with a designer and the first drawing will already be about your cafe.
